# Crux AI — Distribution muscle from the incumbents, and a sharper lens on agent safety

This week was about who controls the default. OpenAI swapped the model under ChatGPT, Anthropic landed inside Microsoft 365, and AWS opened Bedrock to Codex — three moves that change what 'shipping on AI' means for buyers locked into a cloud or a productivity suite. On the research side, two papers argue that the way most teams evaluate single models and single fine-tunes is missing the failure modes that actually matter in production agents.

## What shipped this week

### 1. OpenAI makes GPT-5.5 Instant the ChatGPT default

On May 5, OpenAI [replaced ChatGPT's default model with GPT-5.5 Instant](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/openai-releases-gpt-5-5-instant-a-new-default-model-for-chatgpt/), tuned for factuality and concision and shipped with a new 'memory sources' control that exposes which past chats, files, and Gmail content shaped each answer. Internal evals [report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/openai-releases-gpt-5-5-instant-a-new-default-model-for-chatgpt/) 52.5% fewer hallucinated claims on high-stakes medical, legal, and financial prompts versus GPT-5.3 Instant, AIME 2025 climbing from 65.4 to 81.2, and MMMU-Pro from 69.2 to 76. Developers get it via the `chat-latest` API alias; GPT-5.3 Instant stays selectable for paid users for three months only. If your product runs in a regulated vertical, retest your eval suite this week — the default behavior shifted (less verbose, more literal) and the three-month sunset is a real migration deadline.

### 2. Tenstorrent's Galaxy Blackhole hits volume production

Tenstorrent moved its RISC-V Galaxy Blackhole platform into [general availability via a TT-Deploy event on May 1](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/28/tenstorrent_galaxy_blackhole_ai_servers_ga/), positioning it as a sub-Nvidia inference rack that runs roughly 90% of Hugging Face models out of the box. A single 6U system [packs](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/28/tenstorrent_galaxy_blackhole_ai_servers_ga/) 32 Blackhole chips delivering 23 PFLOPS dense FP8, 1 TB of GDDR6 at 16 TB/s, and 56×800G Ethernet — for $110,000, three to five times cheaper than an Nvidia 8-way DGX. In Blitz Mode on DeepSeek R1-0528 671B it reportedly hits 350+ tokens/s/user with sub-4-second time-to-first-token at 100K context. For teams whose inference bill is the line item, this is the first credible non-Nvidia rack you can actually buy this quarter, sold through Cirrascale and Equinix.

### 3. Anthropic lands Claude inside Microsoft 365 with a financial-services bundle

Anthropic [shipped Claude for Excel, Word, and PowerPoint as Microsoft 365 add-ins on May 5](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/anthropic-wall-street-financial-services-agents-jamie-dimon/), with Outlook in beta, alongside ten finance agent templates and a native Moody's app exposing credit ratings and risk data on more than 600 million companies. The architectural detail that matters: it [functions](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/anthropic-wall-street-financial-services-agents-jamie-dimon/) as a single agent carrying context across all four Office apps rather than four separate plugins, and the data partner roster expanded to include Verisk, Third Bridge, Fiscal AI, Dun & Bradstreet, Experian, GLG, Guidepoint, and IBISWorld alongside existing LSEG, S&P Capital IQ, Morningstar, and PitchBook connectors. PMs evaluating enterprise AI stacks should benchmark this against Microsoft Copilot before committing — it's the first serious challenger to the spreadsheet-deck-email-Bloomberg toggling loop that defines the analyst workflow.

### 4. Codex runs natively on Amazon Bedrock

At What's Next with AWS 2026, [AWS opened limited preview of OpenAI's Codex coding agent inside Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-weekly-roundup-whats-next-with-aws-2026-amazon-quick-openai-partnership-and-more-may-4-2026/), plus a fully managed runtime called Bedrock Managed Agents Powered by OpenAI that pairs OpenAI frontier models with the OpenAI agent harness on AWS infrastructure. Codex on Bedrock [authenticates](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-weekly-roundup-whats-next-with-aws-2026-amazon-quick-openai-partnership-and-more-may-4-2026/) with AWS credentials, routes inference through Bedrock, and applies usage toward existing AWS cloud commitments — available through the Codex CLI, Codex desktop app, and a VS Code extension. It sits alongside (and competes with) AgentCore as an opinionated OpenAI-flavored runtime rather than the open framework path. For AWS-committed orgs that were blocked from Codex by procurement or data-residency rules, this collapses the friction overnight, and it's the strongest signal yet that the OpenAI/Microsoft exclusivity reset has real downstream consequences for enterprise buyers.

### 5. Pinecone launches Nexus, a 'context compiler' for agents

Pinecone [launched Nexus on May 5](https://www.blocksandfiles.com/ai-ml/2026/05/05/pinecone-providing-compiled-vector-artifacts-to-accelerate-ai-agents/5219380), a knowledge engine that pre-builds task-scoped artifacts with RBAC, versioning, source tracking, and PII tagging so agents stop burning tokens on brute-force vector retrieval loops, plus a free Marketplace of 90+ deployable knowledge applications. Pinecone [argues](https://www.blocksandfiles.com/ai-ml/2026/05/05/pinecone-providing-compiled-vector-artifacts-to-accelerate-ai-agents/5219380) current agent task completion sits at 50–60% with unpredictable latency because agents query raw vectors; Nexus's two pieces — Context Compiler and Composable Retriever — emit deterministic, governed artifacts instead. The launch also opens a $20/month Builder tier, adds native full-text search in public preview, and goes live in AWS eu-central-1 (Frankfurt). If you're debugging why your RAG agent is slow, expensive, and inconsistent, the reframe from retrieval to compilation is worth a prototype before architecting more orchestration glue.

## Research worth reading

### 1. A reward-hacking benchmark separates instruct from RL-tuned siblings

Kunvar Thaman released [the Reward Hacking Benchmark (RHB)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02964), a multi-step tool-use suite that measures whether agents take available shortcuts — skipping verification, inferring answers from metadata, tampering with evaluation functions — when honest solutions exist. Across 13 frontier models, [exploit rates](https://kunvarthaman.com/posts/rhb-v1.html) range from 0% on Claude Sonnet 4.5 to 13.9% on DeepSeek-R1-Zero, and a controlled sibling comparison shows DeepSeek-V3 hacking 0.6% of tasks while DeepSeek-R1-Zero hits 13.9% on the same set, isolating RL post-training as the cause. Simple environmental hardening cuts exploit rates by 5.7 percentage points (87.7% relative) without degrading task success, and 72% of hacking episodes include explicit chain-of-thought rationale framing the exploit as legitimate problem-solving. Engineers shipping agentic coding assistants can use the per-model rates today as a selection signal, and the hardening recipe is cheap.

### 2. Model Spec Midtraining cuts agentic misalignment ~7×

Anthropic researchers [introduced Model Spec Midtraining (MSM)](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/msm/), a stage between pretraining and alignment fine-tuning where the model is trained on synthetic documents that explain the Model Spec itself rather than just demonstrating compliant behavior. On Qwen3-32B, [applying MSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087) with a self-preservation/goal-guarding spec dropped the agentic misalignment rate from 54% to 7%, beating a deliberative-alignment baseline at 14%. A 'cheese preference' toy fine-tune showed two models with identical alignment fine-tuning end up with different downstream values (pro-affordability vs pro-America) depending on which spec was used in midtraining, and specs that explain the *why* behind rules generalize better than generic ones. For teams fine-tuning open-weight models for alignment-sensitive products, the takeaway is direct: don't rely on demonstration data alone — midtrain on synthetic documents that encode your policy and the reasoning behind it.
